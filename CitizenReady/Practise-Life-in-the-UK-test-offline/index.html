How to practise for the Life in the UK test without an internet connection

You can study for the Life in the UK test almost anywhere if you prepare your material before you leave home. Most of the time you lose is spent waiting: on a bus, on a train, in a queue. A plan that does not need a signal turns that time into revision.

Step 1: get your study material onto your phone first

Anything that loads from a website will fail when your signal drops. Before you travel, make sure your handbook, notes or practice questions are stored on the device itself. Check by switching on airplane mode and opening them. If they open, you are ready.

Step 2: study one chapter at a time

The test covers several topics from the handbook. Work through them one at a time instead of jumping between them. Short sessions of ten to fifteen minutes are easier to fit into a journey and easier to remember.

Step 3: write down what you get wrong

A wrong answer is useful. Note the topic, then read the explanation until you can say it in your own words. Come back to the same topic a day or two later and see whether it stuck.

Step 4: add timed mock exams

Once the chapters feel familiar, practise under time pressure with full mock exams of 24 questions. The aim is to get used to reading each question carefully and not rushing. Do not memorise answer positions. Questions can be worded differently, so you need to understand the fact behind each one.

Step 5: check the details with the source

Rules, dates and figures change. Before you book, read the current information on the government site: gov.uk/life-in-the-uk-test. That is also where you book the test.

A checklist for study with no signal

  • Material is stored on your device and opens in airplane mode.
  • You have a chapter list and have ticked off what you covered.
  • You keep a short list of questions you got wrong.
  • You take a timed mock exam once a week or so.
  • You check the current rules before booking.
