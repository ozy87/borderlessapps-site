Life in the UK test practice without a subscription or ads

You can prepare for the Life in the UK test without paying monthly and without putting up with ads. What matters is picking practice material that suits how you study, then using it in a sensible order.

What to check before you pick a practice tool

  • How you pay. Some tools charge weekly or monthly and keep charging after you have passed. Check whether the price is a single payment or a recurring one, and whether you can cancel easily.
  • Ads. Ads in the middle of a practice session break your focus. Some can also link to sites you did not mean to visit. Read the store listing and recent reviews for complaints.
  • Offline use. If the tool needs a connection to load questions, it will fail on a train or with weak signal. Check that it works with no connection.
  • Out-of-date questions. The test is based on a government handbook. Ask where the questions come from, and when they were last checked. Reviews that mention out-of-date questions are a warning sign.
  • Explanations. A score alone does not teach you much. You learn more when each wrong answer is explained.

A simple way to study

  1. Read one handbook chapter at a time.
  2. Practise questions on that chapter straight away, while it is fresh.
  3. Write down the questions you got wrong and read the explanation for each.
  4. Once you have covered every chapter, take full mock exams. The test has 24 questions, so use mocks of the same length to get used to the format.
  5. Repeat the mocks until your wrong answers are rare and not clustered in one topic.

Book the test on the government site only

Practice apps do not book your test. Use gov.uk/life-in-the-uk-test for booking, current rules and what to bring on the day.

Where CitizenReady fits
